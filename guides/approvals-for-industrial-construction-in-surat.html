This Is A General Overview, Not A Rule Book

Requirements for industrial construction can depend on the plot, the use, the local authority, the project scale and many other factors. We do not keep a list of rules here and we do not state any rule as fact. The safe approach is to verify your specific situation with the relevant authority and a qualified consultant.

At Prachi Construction Projects, our role is civil construction. We can help coordinate drawings and site work once the owner has the required guidance, but we do not replace a consultant or an authority decision. If anything in this guide conflicts with written guidance you receive, follow the written guidance.

What this guide will and will not do

It will tell you who to ask, what questions to prepare and how to keep a clear record. It will not tell you that a particular permission, form, fee, clearance or timeline applies to your plot. Those points are [CONFIRM WITH OWNER] because they must be verified for your case.

We deliberately keep this guide free of checklists that claim to be complete. A short list can give false confidence when the plot, use or process is different. The safer habit is to ask the right people the right questions and keep their written answers with the project file.

Who To Verify With In Surat

Owners often hear several authority names and assume they all apply in the same way. That assumption is risky. Depending on your location and project, you may need to check with SMC, SUDA, GIDC or GPCB, among others. We will not say which one has jurisdiction over your plot; please verify that directly.

How to approach each verification

  • Ask SMC about the particular site and what applies to it.
  • Ask SUDA where relevant for the location and use proposed.
  • Ask GIDC if the plot is in an industrial estate setting.
  • Ask GPCB about environmental clearances or consents that may be relevant to the process.

These are suggestions for questions, not statements of requirement. A qualified consultant should confirm the correct path before you commit to a drawing or a construction programme.

It is also useful to ask each authority whether another department or office is relevant to your case. Names and responsibilities can vary by location and project type. We do not summarise those boundaries here because they must be confirmed for the specific plot. Treat every answer as project-specific.

Prepare Questions, Not Assumptions

Before approaching an authority, prepare a short description of the plot, the proposed use, the building form and the process involved. Carry a location sketch and any available drawing. Ask what applies, what should be submitted and who will confirm it in writing. Do not rely on a verbal answer given in passing.

Owners sometimes ask us whether a particular ceiling, setback or floor area is allowed. We do not answer from memory. Those details are [CONFIRM WITH OWNER / STRUCTURAL ENGINEER] and must be checked against the approved drawing and the applicable guidance for that plot.

If a service connection is involved, ask separately how the interface should be shown on the civil drawing. An unplanned opening or chamber can affect the floor and drainage layout later.

Keep the questions short and factual. Describe the plot, the proposed building use and the process in plain language, then ask what the authority needs to see. A clear question usually leads to a clearer answer and reduces the chance of acting on an assumption.

Use A Qualified Consultant

A qualified consultant can interpret the requirements for your specific use and location and put the position in writing. That written guidance is more useful than a collection of anecdotes from other units. It also gives the designer and contractor a common reference when drawings are prepared.

We work from approved drawings and coordinate civil work with the owner’s designer and other specialists. We do not certify compliance. Where a technical value or compliance statement is needed, we mark it [CONFIRM WITH OWNER / STRUCTURAL ENGINEER] or [CONFIRM WITH OWNER] and ask the responsible person to provide it.

What to give the consultant

Give them the plot details, proposed use, machine and process information, a draft layout and any correspondence you already have. Clear inputs reduce the chance of a late change after construction begins.

Ask the consultant to put the basis of their advice in writing, including any points that remain open. That document helps the designer and contractor avoid filling gaps with their own assumptions. It also gives the owner a reference when a question arises during construction.

Keep A Simple Verification Record

Maintain one file with the drawings, written clarifications, consultant advice and authority correspondence. Note the date of each document and keep superseded versions clearly marked. This helps the site team use the current drawing and gives the owner a trail if a question arises later.

On site, we use the same discipline for revisions. When a drawing changes, we confirm what has changed before work continues. If a verification is still pending, we record it rather than assume it will arrive in time.

A simple file structure is enough: one folder for drawings, one for written clarifications and one for consultant advice. Mark the current drawing clearly. When a clarification changes a layout, tell the designer before the site team changes the work.

Owner Checklist Before Work Starts

Before mobilisation, confirm that you have written guidance for the plot and use, an approved drawing set, a named consultant, and clarity on any service or process interface. If any of these are open, pause and resolve them. Starting early can create rework that no contractor can fully undo later.
